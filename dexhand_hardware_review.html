01

Orientation — the design axes that actually matter

Before the per-entry detail, four axes separate these hands from each other. "How many DOF" is no longer a clean proxy for capability.

AXIS 01

Actuation topology

DIRECT / QDD motors at the joint — clean mapping, backdrivable, but bulky & hot in-hand. LINKAGE few motors, coupled joints — cheap & strong, hard mapping. TENDON motors relocated to palm/base — most anthropomorphic, but friction, hysteresis & calibration.

AXIS 02

Degrees of freedom

From 6 active (Inspire) through the 12–22 "human-equivalent" band, up to 27 (Clone) and Daxo's 108–120 actuators. More DOF trades against reliability, cost, controllability and data efficiency.

AXIS 03

Tactile & sensing stack

Increasingly the real differentiator: fingertip-only vs full-palm arrays, taxel density, force resolution, and whether visuo-tactile (camera-in-finger) is included.

AXIS 04

Product philosophy

A genuine fault line: maximal hardware (push DOF, sensing, anthropomorphism) versus data-centric minimalism (simple cheap hand, win on a scaled data engine and the model).

03

Standalone dexterous-hand specialists

Sharpa — SharpaWaveSingapore · sharpa.com ↗

22 DOFsensing-first

A 1:1 human-scale, 22-active-DOF hand whose pitch is high-resolution touch. The proprietary Dynamic Tactile Array claims >1,000 px/fingertip, sub-mm resolution, pressure sensitivity ~0.005–0.02 N over 0–30 N, and 180 FPS — enabling texture recognition and 6D force. Mass production from Oct 2025, open ROS2 stack, paired with CraftNet (Vision-Tactile-Language-Action, "System 0" reflex layer). Demoed at IROS 2025, CES 2026 honoree, integrated into the NVIDIA Isaac GR00T reference humanoid.

Wuji Tech — Wuji HandShenzhen · ex-Pan-Motor · wuji.tech ↗

20 DOFdirect-drive

Vertically integrated on actuation. 20 active DOF, four joints per finger, direct-drive micro-actuators in the phalanges (no tendon slack). Reported ~0.6–0.9 kg, ~15 N fingertip, ~10 kg static grasp, ~1 kHz control, 300,000+ grasp cycles, ~80 cm drop survival. Companion glove: 526 touch points @ 120 FPS. Strategically the hardware partner behind Genesis AI's "Genesis Hand 1.0" (3 ms latency, tuned for human-data collection into the GENE model).

RobotEra — XHAND1Beijing · robotera.com ↗

12 DOFQDD

12 active DOF (3 on thumb & index, 2 on the rest), full-gear quasi-direct-drive with decoupled, backdrivable joints — explicitly marketed as a clean platform for IL/RL. Index swings ±15°, thumb reaches pinky opposition. Tactile: >100-point per-fingertip array, 270° encircling 3D force + temperature. Whole-hand grip ~80 N. Integrates with ERA-42 and supports Apple Vision Pro teleop. Slimmer XHAND Lite (11 DOF) for service robots.

Inspire — RH56 seriesBeijing · 因时机器人 · inspire-robots.com ↗

6 DOFlinkage

The incumbent research workhorse. 6 active DOF / 12 joints via six miniature linear servos, underactuated & coupled. Variants: BFX (fast "pianist"), DFX (balanced; ~15 N thumb), all-metal flagship F1 (EtherCAT 1 kHz, 24 sensors, 30 kg static). Ubiquitous on Unitree G1/H1. But a 2026 characterization paper (Tan, Xie, Correll) documents its limits as an instrument: uncalibrated proprioception, up to +1618% force overshoot at speed, non-trivial antipodal grasps. Essential cautionary reading.

BrainCo / Qiangnao — Revo seriesHangzhou · 强脑科技 · brainco.tech ↗

11 / 21 DOFvisuo-tactile

From BCI and prosthetics into robot hands. Revo 2: 11 DOF, 383 g, 50 N, 20 kg load, RS485/CAN-FD/EtherCAT @ 1 kHz, Basic/Pro/Touch tiers. Revo 3 (2026): a 21-DOF jump to direct-drive backdrivable with full-palm tactile + fingertip visuo-tactile + 70 N grip — positioned as the first 20+ DOF open-ecosystem hand combining those modes. Demoed 33 grasp types, two-handed Rubik's cube. HK IPO reportedly in motion.

Linkerbot — Linker HandBeijing · 灵心巧手 · linkerbot.cn ↗

6–42 DOFall routes

Volume leader and the most explicit "embrace all routes" strategy (founder Zhou Yong, 2023). L6 (6 DOF direct, ~¥19,900), L20 (20–21 DOF linkage, ~¥49,900), L30 (25 DOF tendon, ~¥100k), research line to 42 DOF. Pricing ~1/10–1/20 of overseas. Reported 10,000+ units in 2025, >80% of China's high-DOF market, ~¥1.5B Series B at >¥10B valuation. Customers: Stanford, Cambridge, PKU, Tsinghua. The clearest lab-to-standard-product case study.

Agile Robots — Agile HandMunich + China · agile-robots.com ↗

15–16 DOFcompliant

Founded 2018, acquired Franka Emika (2023) — force-control arm pedigree. 21 joints / 15–16 DOF, 1.5 kg, ~10 N fingertip, 360°/s, with identical modular fingers (actuator + electronics + sensing + transmission in each). Joint-torque + fingertip tactile, active compliant control for safe contact. Now embedded in the Agile ONE industrial humanoid.

AgiBot / Zhiyuan — OmniHandShanghai · 智元 · agibot.com ↗

10–22 DOFmodular

Modular end-effector family on a standard wrist (swaps with OmniPicker). OmniHand 2025: 10 active + 6 passive DOF, ~500 g, 400+ taxels @ 0.1 N, open SDK. Pro: 19 DOF (12 active), 35–60 N, 150+ sensors. OmniHand 3 Ultra-T (Apr 2026): 22+3 DOF, tendon-driven, full-hand 3D tactile, 10:1 load-to-weight. Presented at MWC 2026; tied to a European manufacturing partnership.

Daxo Robotics — Muscle V0Austin · Tom Zhang ↗ · daxo-robotics.com ↗

108–120 act.soft redundant

The contrarian. A soft, tendon-driven hand with 108–120 actuators (~3× the human hand), pitched as "infinite DOF." Explicitly rejects the human hand as a reference — dexterity comes from control complexity, not joint count. Redundancy is the headline: the AI reroutes through remaining muscles and reportedly keeps working with ~20% of motors failed. Electronics live outside the hand. Built cheap (~$1,200, ~$5 motors), assembled in hours. Targets Q3 2026 with industrial pilots.

early stage

Origami RoboticsYC · US

earlydata-first

Early-stage; thesis is the data problem. Large transmissions make current hands hard to model, and the embodiment gap inflates the train/deploy distribution. Their answer: build both the hand and a co-designed data-collection glove, optimizing miniature motors so glove kinematics match the hand — minimizing sim-to-real and embodiment gaps for scalable high-quality data. Aligned with the broader "hardware in service of data" pattern.

Xiaomi — CyberOne V2 handin-house · mi.com ↗

22–27 DOFliquid-cooled

The 2026 redesign reaches 22–27 DOF (~83% jump in active DOF), volume cut ~60% to a true 1:1 adult-hand scale (improving sim-to-real for teleop data). The standout is thermal: "bionic sweat glands" — 3D-printed metal liquid-cooling channels evaporating ~0.5 ml/min to dissipate ~100 W from compact motors, attacking one of the hardest constraints in dense hands. Full-palm tactile, 150,000+ cycles. Edge: a captive customer in Xiaomi's own EV factory.

Figure AI — Figure 03 handHelix-02 · figure.ai ↗

16 DOF3 g tactile

16-DOF five-finger hands; the emphasis is compliance + sensing, not DOF. Softer, larger-surface fingertips, a small palm-embedded camera for occluded close-range feedback, and an in-house tactile fingertip sensor sensitive to ~3 grams (a paperclip) to predict slip before it happens. Engineered around the Helix / Helix-02 VLA (pixels-to-action, onboard, 44 DOF total). Vertically integrated, BotQ ~12,000 units/yr, ~$20k target, TIME Best Invention 2025.

Boston Dynamics — Atlas GR grippersUS · bostondynamics.com ↗

7 DOFnon-anthropomorphic

A deliberately non-anthropomorphic path. GR2: 7-DOF, three-finger (two fingers + opposable swiveling thumb), tactile + palm camera, and a superhuman trick — fingers bend fully backward for back-of-hand grasps. Philosophy: "the fewest fingers that achieve complex manipulation," reliability over biomimicry. Plot twist: at CES 2026 BD revealed a more conventional three-finger hand — a concession to human-tool compatibility. Atlas runs Large Behavior Models; in pilots at a Hyundai plant.

no photo

Sunday Robotics — MemoMountain View · Zhao + Chi · sunday.ai ↗

pincerdata-maximal

The purest "hardware minimalism, data maximalism" bet — founders Tony Zhao (ALOHA/ACT) and Cheng Chi (Diffusion Policy/UMI). A wheeled two-arm home robot with intentionally simple pincer "Lego-like" hands. The product is the data engine: a patented Skill Capture Glove matching Memo's hands, worn across 500+ homes to produce ~10 million chore episodes feeding the in-house ACT-1 model, emphasizing zero-shot generalization to unseen homes. The implicit argument: for many home tasks, matched-embodiment data beats end-effector dexterity.

05

Frontier research, open-source & biomimetic

The part the field actually reads — and where the most useful hardware ideas often originate before commercialization.

Benchmarks & commercial-research crossoverShadow ↗ · Allegro ↗ · PSYONIC ↗

Shadow Dexterous Hand (London, since 2005): 24 joints, 20 DOF, tendon-driven, extensive tactile — the long-standing gold standard, historically >$100k and fragile, the exact accessibility gap the open-source hands attack. Allegro Hand: direct-drive, 16 DOF, ~15,000 CHF — the default RL/sim2real platform (e.g. NVIDIA's DeXtreme with Isaac Gym). PSYONIC Ability Hand: a bionic prosthesis adapted for robotics (490 g, closes ~0.2 s, lower native DOF), on Apptronik Apollo and Hexagon AEON — the "modular, simple first" humanoid philosophy.

Open-source hands — the accessibility waveLEAP ↗ · ORCA ↗ · RUKA ↗ · MM-Hand

tendondirect

LEAP Hand (CMU; Shaw, Pathak): open-source, direct-drive, 16 DOF, <$2,000, ~3 hr assembly — a landmark in cheap reproducible research. ORCA Hand (ETH SRL; 2504.04259): tendon-driven, 17 DOF (16 fingers + 1 wrist), integrated tactile, <2,000 CHF, assembled in under 8 hours, with popping joints, auto-calibration & tensioning — matches LEAP accuracy/latency despite tendons. RUKA / Ruka-v2 (NYU; 2603.26660): 11 DOF, <$1,300, learns tendon dynamics directly; v2 adds a 2-DOF parallel wrist + finger abduction. MM-Hand: 21-DOF multimodal modular, remote tendon actuation to free finger volume and keep heat out.

Biomimetic / muscle-driven — the high-fidelity frontierClone ↗ · Faive/Mimic ↗

27 DOFrolling-contact

Clone Robotics: anthropomorphic 27-DOF hand with artificial-muscle actuation, claiming human-level grip strength & speed; its Neural Joint V2 controller is a network trained on human-hand video rather than hardcoded control. Faive Hand / Mimic Robotics (ETH spinout): biomimetic, tendon-driven with rolling-contact joints (no fixed axis) — closed-loop RL trained in Isaac Gym, transferred to hardware; rolling joints break the usual encoder assumptions. Historical grounding: the ACT Hand, the Biomimetic Hand (9 actuators), and CasiaHand (15 DOF, palm-integrated).

Wearable / data-collection hardwareadjacent but central

The "hand as data device" pattern recurs: Wuji's 526-point glove, Origami's matched glove, Sunday's Skill Capture Glove, and academic work like the HIRO Hand (a wearable robotic hand for hand-over-hand imitation that captures the operator's own tactile feedback). For a VLA-eval context this matters — the glove is the embodiment that defines the training distribution.

06

Cross-cutting themes

1

The DOF "arms race" is bifurcating

One camp pushes toward and past human DOF (Clone 27, Xiaomi 22–27, Linker research 42, Daxo 108+). The opposite camp argues DOF is the wrong axis and bets on data + model (Sunday, Boston Dynamics, PSYONIC-on-Apollo). Both have credible 2026 traction.

2

Tactile is the new battleground

Sharpa (>1,000 px/fingertip), Figure (3 g sensor), Xiaomi/BrainCo/AgiBot (full-palm arrays), plus palm-camera visuo-tactile. Fingertip-only sensing is becoming table stakes; full-hand 3D tactile is the frontier.

3

Actuation converges on "motors out of the fingers"

Remote tendon (MM-Hand, Daxo, OmniHand 3) and palm-located actuation address the two killers of in-finger motors: inertia and heat. Xiaomi's liquid cooling is the brute-force alternative for those who keep motors in-hand.

4

Reliability & characterization gate research use

The Inspire RH56DFX paper is the cautionary tale: catalog DOF and force say little about overshoot, calibration, and contact behavior. ORCA's whole pitch is reliability-as-feature.

5

China dominates volume; the West leads integrated stacks

Linkerbot, Inspire, BrainCo, AgiBot, RobotEra, Wuji, Xiaomi own the cost curve and unit volume. Figure, Boston Dynamics, Sunday, Apptronik, Daxo, Clone integrate the hand into a learning-first robot. Agile and Sharpa straddle both.

6

The hand is co-designed with the model

SharpaWave/CraftNet, Figure/Helix, RobotEra/ERA-42, Sunday/ACT-1, Wuji/Genesis Hand. Hardware and policy are no longer separable — the data-collection embodiment is part of the spec.

08

Notes on methodology & gaps

How to read DOF and force numbers. "Active DOF" vs "total joints" vs "passive DOF" are reported inconsistently (Inspire's "6 DOF / 12 joints," AgiBot's "10 active + 6 passive," Agile's "21 joints / 15–16 DOF"). Force figures mix fingertip, thumb, whole-hand, and static-load. Treat all single-number comparisons as approximate until characterized on a common bench.

Coverage gaps to consider adding: Tesla Optimus Gen 3 hand (~22 DOF, in-house, not publicly specced), Sanctuary AI Phoenix, Unitree's own Dex hands, DeepRobotics OmniHand2025 (16–19 DOF), RoboSense Papert 2.0 (20 DOF, lidar-vendor crossover), and Dexmate / DexRobot in the Chinese long tail. If this review feeds an eval/benchmark scoping exercise, the most decision-relevant missing axis is standardized contact/force characterization (the Inspire paper is the template) — that, not catalog DOF, determines whether a given hand can serve as a reliable eval substrate.
